# Brand assets as visual identification — legal and product review

Status: documentary review only. This document is not legal advice, does not
authorize publishing any third-party asset, and does not change the Brand Asset
Registry or its rights gate.

Review date: 2026-09-21

## 1. FutScout context and exact proposed use

FutScout is an independent football scouting and player-research platform
focused on EA SPORTS FC Career Mode and real-world football. It is not
affiliated with or endorsed by EA SPORTS, API-Sports, a club, league, or
federation.

The proposed use is limited to visual identification of an entity, for example:

```text
[club crest] Real Madrid
[competition logo] LaLiga
```

FutScout does not intend to sell or sublicense the artwork, manufacture
merchandise, alter the marks, adopt them as FutScout branding, use them in its
own advertising, or present a joint brand, sponsorship, partnership, or
endorsement.

That narrower purpose matters, but it does not by itself create a general
permission. Names, figurative trade marks, copyright in artwork, contractual
source restrictions, and the risk of confusion are separate questions.

## 2. Executive conclusion

There is a legally relevant concept of referential or identifying use in
Brazil, the European Union, and the United Kingdom. The protection is not
absolute and its boundaries differ by jurisdiction.

The strongest factors in FutScout's favour are factual identification, small
and proportionate presentation, placement next to the exact entity name,
absence of merchandise or logo resale, visual separation from FutScout
branding, and a conspicuous non-affiliation notice.

The principal risks are:

- Brazil's express statutory limitation for citation in a publication is
  conditioned on the absence of commercial connotation;
- a logo is more distinctive than a name and may also be protected as an
  artistic work under copyright law;
- advertising or subscriptions make the use more clearly commercial, even if
  the logo itself is not sold;
- prominent or repeated card/catalog presentation can suggest an official
  relationship or take advantage of a mark's reputation;
- hotlinking does not eliminate trade-mark display risk, while caching and CDN
  storage add reproduction and contractual questions;
- some rightsholders publish policies requiring express permission. The
  Premier League, for example, expressly says its logos require permission and
  that clubs retain their own marks.

Therefore the six proposed pilot assets must remain `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. A
future pilot is reasonable only after asset-specific source authorization and
specialist legal review for the intended territories and monetization model.

## 3. Analytical method

Each conclusion below separates:

- **Documented rule:** wording from legislation, a court, regulator, official
  rightsholder, or source-provider terms.
- **Interpretation for FutScout:** application to the proposed interface.
- **Residual risk:** uncertainty that cannot be removed by product design.
- **Conservative recommendation:** operational rule for the rights gate.

Public practices of other football sites are market/UX observations only.
They do not establish whether those sites have licences or whether the same
legal position applies to FutScout.

## 4. Brazil

### 4.1 Trade marks

**Documented rule.** Article 129 of Law 9,279/1996 grants the registrant
exclusive use of the mark throughout Brazil. Article 130 protects its material
integrity and reputation. Article 132 contains limits, including use to indicate
the purpose of an accessory under honest competition practices and citation in
a speech, scientific or literary work, or another publication when it has no
commercial connotation and does not harm the mark's distinctive character.
Article 195 also addresses unfair competition, including fraudulent diversion
of customers and use of another party's advertising sign in a way that creates
confusion.

Official source: [Brazilian Industrial Property Law — Law 9,279/1996](https://planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm).

**Interpretation for FutScout.** Textual club and competition names used as
database labels have a stronger factual/referential character than a prominent
figurative crest. However, Article 132(IV)'s express condition concerning no
commercial connotation makes reliance on that provision uncertain once the
site carries advertising, subscriptions, or other revenue.

The STJ has accepted, in a specific dispute involving authorized resale of ABNT
technical standards, reference to ABNT's word and figurative marks solely to
identify the origin of the standards. That precedent is useful evidence that
Brazilian law can recognize necessary origin-identifying use, including a logo,
in a commercial context. It is not a general authorization for sports logos:
the reseller's underlying right to commercialize the identified product was
important to the case.

Official source: [STJ — reference to ABNT word and figurative marks to indicate origin](https://www.stj.jus.br/sites/portalp/Paginas/Comunicacao/Noticias-antigas/2018/2018-02-15_09-13_Confirmado-a-terceiro-o-direito-de-vender-normas-tecnicas-com-a-marca-ABNT.aspx).

**Residual risk.** FutScout supplies its own information service rather than
reselling an authorized club product. Famous football marks can be protected
strongly, and use must not suggest that the service originates from, is
licensed by, or is endorsed by the rightsholder.

### 4.2 Copyright and logos

**Documented rule.** Law 9,610/1998 protects drawings and other artistic works.
Article 29 generally requires prior express authorization for reproduction,
storage in a computer, and other uses. Article 8 excludes isolated names and
titles from copyright protection, although trade-mark and unfair-competition
rules may still apply. Article 46 contains limited exceptions, including
quotation and certain reproductions justified by purpose and without
unjustified prejudice to the author's legitimate interests.

Official source: [Brazilian Copyright Law — Law 9,610/1998](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm).

The STJ has recognized that an original logo may qualify as a protected
intellectual work. Official source: [STJ precedent concerning copyright in a
logo](https://www.stj.jus.br/docs_internet/revista/eletronica/stj-revista-eletronica-1997_100.pdf).

**Interpretation for FutScout.** A text name and a graphic crest are not
equivalent. The isolated name is not copyright-protected as such, while the
crest may combine trade-mark, copyright, and design interests. Persistent use
of the full crest as an interface identifier is not clearly covered by a broad
Brazilian copyright exception.

**Conservative recommendation.** In Brazil, textual names are the safer
fallback. Do not publish crests or competition logos on the basis of
referential purpose alone without specialist review or written permission.

## 5. European Union

### 5.1 Referential trade-mark use

**Documented rule.** Article 14 of Directive (EU) 2015/2436 permits use of a
trade mark in the course of trade to identify or refer to goods or services as
those of the proprietor, provided the use complies with honest practices in
industrial or commercial matters. The parallel EU trade-mark rule appears in
Article 14 of Regulation (EU) 2017/1001.

Official sources:

- [Directive (EU) 2015/2436, Article 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32015L2436)
- [Regulation (EU) 2017/1001](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32017R1001)

**Interpretation for FutScout.** Necessary and honest identification is a
recognized legal category even in commercial activity. The analysis remains
fact-sensitive: proportionality, necessity, presentation, confusion,
reputational harm, and unfair advantage matter. If a textual name provides
complete identification, use of a highly distinctive crest may be harder to
justify as necessary, although Article 14 is not limited solely to spare parts
or accessories.

### 5.2 Origin, affiliation, and reputation

EU case law treats the origin function of a mark as central. In *Arsenal
Football Club v Reed* (C-206/01), the Court held that a proprietor may prevent
commercial use liable to create the impression of a material link with the
club. A disclaimer did not automatically remove that concern in the product
context of that case.

Official source: [CJEU press release and summary — C-206/01](https://curia.europa.eu/en/actu/communiques/cp02/aff/cp0291en.htm).

**Interpretation for FutScout.** That case concerned merchandise, not a factual
database, so it does not determine FutScout's outcome. It does show that
football marks receive functional trade-mark protection and that a disclaimer
cannot cure presentation that otherwise communicates origin or affiliation.

### 5.3 Copyright remains separate

The trade-mark referential-use limitation does not itself grant copyright in
the artwork. National copyright law and EU harmonized rules may independently
govern reproduction, storage, and communication of the logo.

**Conservative recommendation.** For EU availability, treat a licence or a
documented legal assessment covering both trade mark and artwork as preferable
to relying solely on Article 14.

## 6. United Kingdom

### 6.1 Trade marks and passing off

**Documented rule.** The Trade Marks Act 1994 recognizes use for identifying
goods or services as those of the proprietor, but use outside honest industrial
or commercial practices, or use taking unfair advantage of or harming a mark's
distinctive character or reputation, can infringe. UK law also protects
unregistered marks through passing off where goodwill, misrepresentation, and
damage are established.

Official sources:

- [Trade Marks Act 1994](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/26/contents)
- [UK IPO — using somebody else's trade mark](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/trade-marks)
- [UK government guidance on unregistered marks and passing off](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/technical-guidance-for-official-receivers/39-intellectual-property-and-other-intangible-assets)

The UK IPO's conservative public guidance is to contact the owner and obtain a
licence when using another party's trade mark.

### 6.2 Football-specific policy evidence

The Premier League states that its logos and trade marks cannot be used without
express permission and that its clubs retain their own marks and should be
contacted before reproduction of names or badges.

Official source: [Premier League business proposal and trademark FAQ](https://www.premierleague.com/en/about/faq/other).

**Interpretation for FutScout.** Whatever general referential-use arguments
might exist, this published policy makes an unlicensed Premier League pilot a
poor conservative choice. It also demonstrates why permission from a league
must not be presumed to cover club crests.

**Conservative recommendation.** Obtain express permission for the Premier
League logo and each relevant club crest, or a written licence that clearly
covers them collectively.

## 7. Advertising and indirect commercial use

Advertising does not make every referential use automatically unlawful in the
EU or UK. Both systems contemplate some honest referential use in the course of
trade. It does, however, make the activity commercial and increases scrutiny of
necessity, proportionality, unfair advantage, and possible affiliation.

In Brazil, advertising materially weakens reliance on Article 132(IV) because
that provision expressly refers to citation without commercial connotation.
Whether a specific informational page carrying general advertising has the
prohibited commercial connotation is a fact-sensitive legal question, not an
automatic yes or no.

Practical conclusion: advertising changes the risk, even when the logo itself
is not sold. Ads must be visually separated from third-party marks, and a mark
must never appear to endorse an advertiser or paid FutScout feature.

## 8. API-Football as the technical source

The preferred technical path remains:

```text
verified Club/League identity
  -> API-Football metadata
  -> Brand Asset Registry
  -> rights gate
  -> UI or FutScout fallback
```

API-Football's terms distinguish technical availability from rights. They say
logos and images are supplied for identification and descriptive purposes, but
API-Sports does not own them, grants no commercial competition rights, and
places responsibility for third-party authorization on the user.

Official source: [API-Football terms](https://www.api-football.com/terms).

API-Football also publishes technical guidance recommending that logo files be
downloaded once and cached rather than repeatedly requested from its media CDN.
That is technical/cache guidance, not a trade-mark or copyright licence.

Official source: [API-Football technical guide](https://www.api-football.com/news/post/how-to-get-started-with-api-football-the-complete-beginners-guide).

No wording reviewed expressly prohibits remote display of a logo solely for
identification. Equally, no wording grants the underlying third-party rights.
Therefore:

- `API access` is not `trade-mark/copyright permission`;
- absence of an express hotlink prohibition is not a positive authorization;
- cache guidance does not authorize publication of the cached artwork;
- the source contract and the rightsholder analysis must both pass.

## 9. Remote display, cache, and CDN

Remote display and local storage answer different questions:

| Method | What it may avoid | What it does not avoid | Relative conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote display/hotlink | FutScout retaining its own stored copy | Public display of the mark, confusion, unfair advantage, source terms, availability and withdrawal | Not inherently safe |
| Temporary cache | Repeated source requests | Reproduction/storage questions and public mark use | Requires explicit source and rights review |
| Own storage/CDN | Source instability and performance limits | Reproduction, distribution, update and deletion duties | Higher operational/legal burden |

Remote display can be narrower than permanent storage, but it is not
automatically lawful or safer. Cache/CDN generally requires clearer permission
because FutScout creates and serves a copy. Hotlinking can also violate source
terms, bypass withdrawal controls, or continue displaying an unauthorized mark.

## 10. Product-surface risk matrix

These ratings are relative product-risk classifications, not legal findings.

| Use | Risk | Reasoning | Conservative control |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Textual club/league name | LOWER | Strong factual-identification purpose; no reproduction of artwork | Use exact official name as plain text, never as FutScout branding |
| B. Crest/logo beside exact name | MODERATE–HIGH | Clear referential context, but full distinctive artwork and possible copyright | Small, proportional, unchanged, asset-specific approval |
| C. Crest on Player Card | HIGH | Repeated promotional card surface; greater visual prominence and possible official-data implication | Keep fallback until licensed/reviewed; separate from ads and CTAs |
| D. Crest on club page | MODERATE | Strong entity context but potentially prominent hero treatment | Place beside club name; no co-branding; restrained dimensions |
| E. League logo in catalogue | MODERATE–HIGH | Repeated commercial directory use and distinctive competition branding | Text fallback; permission for each competition |
| F. Player comparison | MODERATE–HIGH | Identification is relevant, but repeated cards can amplify commercial presentation | Minimal logo size and no decorative repetition |
| G. Any of the above with advertising | HIGH in Brazil; MODERATE–HIGH in EU/UK | Commercial connotation/course of trade and potential unfair advantage | Separate ads; no logo-ad association; legal review |
| H. Own cache/CDN | HIGH/UNCLEAR | Adds copying, storage, delivery, update and removal obligations | Require written permission and auditable removal process |
| I. Remote display/hotlink | MODERATE/UNCLEAR | Avoids local copy but not public display or source-contract issues | Require source permission and rightsholder clearance |

## 11. Presentation controls that reduce risk

If an asset is eventually authorized:

- display it only beside or within the clearly named entity context;
- use the minimum size reasonably necessary for identification;
- preserve proportions, colours, clear space, and official artwork;
- do not crop, recolour, animate, stylize, or combine marks;
- keep FutScout branding visually independent;
- never place a crest in the FutScout logo, favicon, domain, app icon, or a
  manner resembling a joint brand;
- do not use third-party marks in FutScout advertising creatives, pricing
  pages, subscription CTAs, or sponsor placements without separate permission;
- prevent ads from appearing as if endorsed by the adjacent club or league;
- do not sell artwork, downloads, templates, or merchandise;
- use accurate accessible text such as `Real Madrid crest`, not promotional
  claims;
- display a clear non-affiliation notice and an IP contact/removal channel;
- preserve asset provenance, permission evidence, territory, expiry, and
  removal obligations;
- immediately restore the FutScout fallback when permission expires or is
  withdrawn.

These controls reduce confusion and overuse. They do not create a licence.

## 13. Market-reference observations

Public football databases commonly place club crests beside team names in
directories, profiles, and tables. Examples reviewed include SoFIFA,
Transfermarkt, FBref, FotMob, and Sofascore.

- SoFIFA publishes a notice stating that names, logos, and brands belong to
  their owners and are used for identification. [SoFIFA terms](https://sofifa.com/help/tos)
- Transfermarkt publicly displays club crests and operates a correction flow,
  but its public pages do not establish the legal basis for FutScout's use.
  [Transfermarkt FAQ](https://www.transfermarkt.com/intern/faq)
- FBref uses crests in contextual club directories. [FBref club directory](https://fbref.com/en/squads/)
- FotMob and Sofascore use similar entity-centred visual patterns, but no
  licence applicable to FutScout was identified in this review.

These examples inform restrained UI design only. They are not legal precedents,
licences, or evidence that the same assets may be copied from those services.

## 14. Rights-status policy

The rights gate must record evidence, not inferred legality:

- `REVIEW_REQUIRED`: default for every third-party crest/logo until the exact
  asset, intended use, source, territory, monetization, and storage method have
  sufficient documented review.
- `REMOTE_ONLY`: use only when both the visual use and remote delivery are
  affirmatively permitted; never create a local/CDN copy.
- `CACHE_ALLOWED`: use only when commercial display and the defined cache or
  storage operation are affirmatively permitted. Record retention, update, and
  deletion conditions.
- `APPROVED`: use only when licence/permission or a documented jurisdictional
  legal assessment covers the actual production use. Approval is asset- and
  scope-specific, not provider-wide.
- `BLOCKED`: use when prohibited, denied, expired, revoked, source-incompatible,
  or incapable of satisfying required conditions.

Technical availability, common industry practice, a public URL, an API
subscription, or a disclaimer cannot promote an asset.

- Keep text names and FutScout fallbacks.
- Complete the API-Sports business/legal outreach already prepared.
- Request permission directly from LaLiga, Premier League, Real Madrid,

  do not require all six to launch together.

### Commercial production

- Prefer written licences or an Order Form that explicitly covers public
  commercial display, advertising/subscriptions, territories, remote delivery,
  cache/CDN, technical transformations, attribution, duration, updates, and
  removal.
- Confirm that the licensor can authorize third-party club and competition
  marks; file access alone is insufficient.
- Obtain local advice for the principal markets. A global website may trigger
  analysis under more than one jurisdiction.
- Maintain an IP notice-and-removal process and periodic rights review.

- FutScout's intended use is identification/information, not sale or
  merchandising of the marks: **confirmed**.

  increases risk and makes the Brazilian statutory citation limitation
  particularly uncertain: **jurisdiction- and fact-dependent**.
- Referential trade-mark use has relevant legal bases in Brazil, the EU, and
  the UK, but none provides a blanket worldwide logo authorization:
  **confirmed with limitations**.
- Names and logos must not be treated identically: **confirmed**.
- A disclaimer is useful but not a substitute for permission: **confirmed**.
- Remote display is not automatically safer than cache in all legal respects:
  **depends on source terms, rights, and implementation**.
- A controlled six-asset policy can be defined, but publication is not yet
  authorized: **confirmed**.
